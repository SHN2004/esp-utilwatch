UtilWatchperfboard case

Print the case

Two printed parts around the one-port perfboard build. The buttons are flexible tabs in the top, and the back plate carries its own posts, so there are no loose parts and no separate posts or spacer.

50.9mm wide
55.1mm 12–6 o'clock
16.9mm thick

Plain PLA or PETG, not carbon fibre. The carbon-fibre filaments that suit this printer are slightly conductive and would choke the ESP32's Bluetooth.

0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 20% infill. Print the fit test before the shell: it shows in 10 minutes what the shell would show in 2 hours.

You also need

  • 2
    M2.5 × 5 mm countersunk screws

    Flat head, sits flush in the back. They go in from the back, into the two 12 o'clock lugs. A hook holds the other end.

  • 1
    Clear sheet, optional

    36 × 21 mm, up to 0.5 mm thick, cut from clear packaging. It sits in a pocket inside the top and keeps sweat off the screen.

  • 1
    20 mm strap

    Your velcro strap threads through the two slots, under the back plate.

Put it together

  1. If you cut a clear sheet, drop it into the pocket inside the top.
  2. Lower the finished board in face down, USB toward the side opening. The screen sits under the window.
  3. Hook the back plate's 6 o'clock lip into the groove in that wall, then swing the plate shut. Its posts land on c1 r1 and c18 r1.
  4. Drive the two screws in gently: they cut their own thread in the plastic.
  5. Thread the strap through both slots.

Measure before printing. The sizes are typical part sizes, not measured from your parts. Check the OLED module (35.4 × 33.5 mm), the tallest point of the slider lever (8 mm above the board's underside) and the bottom of the battery.

Change them at the top of case/perfboard/case.py and run it again. It checks that nothing collides.
